GFF 2026 Demos, Replayed

Six things India's payment gateways demoed in September, rebuilt from my photographs so you can click through them yourself. Every screen is a recreation drawn from what was on stage or at a booth — no real payments, no real accounts, and no company's actual software.

Visa kiosk · processed by PayU

An agent that shops, then pays

The kiosk at Visa's stand ran the whole journey: an AI agent took the request, built a Domino's order, offered saved cards, handed over to PayU, and the shopper approved with a passkey instead of an OTP.

  1. 01Shopper states intent in plain words
  2. 02Agent builds the cart
  3. 03Saved, tokenised cards offered in the chat
  4. 04Handover: "pay securely with PayU"
  5. 05Passkey replaces the OTP
  6. 06Payment verified, order placed
What was real: a demo environment, not a live product. Visa built it with seven Indian gateways — PayU, Razorpay, CCAvenue, Cashfree, Juspay, Paytm and Pine Labs — and three banks. Mastercard ran a comparable authenticated agent payment in India in February 2026, and Cashfree lists an agentic cards launch with Mastercard and Yatra.

Why it matters for us: the card never appears as a number, the shopper never leaves the conversation, and authentication is the only human moment. Whichever network makes that moment easiest for a gateway to wire up gets the agent traffic.

PayU Agent HQ

A score for being found by AI

PayU's booth scored how easily an AI shopper can find a merchant. The sample perfume store scored 49 out of 100: technical readiness counts for 55% of that, and how AI tools see the brand for 45%.

The radar compares the store against its competitors on brand recognition, market presence, presence quality, brand pertinence and share of voice. Then it hands over files that make the store readable by agents.

Why it matters for us: this is a gateway selling growth, not payments. It creates a reason to stay that has nothing to do with pricing — and it is a product idea our acquiring partners could use.

PhonePe × Visa

Three ways a card moves without a card

Tap to Pay: a Visa card saved inside PhonePe, tapped on an ordinary card machine. Launched 9 September for Android, rolling out in phases, built on the Visa card tokens PhonePe added in February 2025.

Smart Accept: the merchant's own phone takes the card tap, with no separate card machine. On stage an Axis Bank Visa card was tapped on an Android phone to pay ₹1,000. Announced, no rollout date.

Scan to Pay abroad: scan a Visa QR code overseas and pay from PhonePe. The demo paid "MTR Singapore". PhonePe says 14 countries, on Visa's network rather than UPI International.

Why it matters for us: Visa is using India's biggest UPI app to put cards where UPI already reaches — including the smallest merchants, who never had a card machine to begin with.

CCAvenue · CommerceAI

From "click to pay" to "chat to pay"

Nova is a store's own assistant: the shopper describes the occasion rather than browsing, and the conversation ends at checkout. At the booth the prompt was about a wedding outfit in a lighter shade.

AI Forms: a merchant describes a payment form in plain words. The assistant builds it through CCAvenue's Remote MCP server and publishes it as a hosted link and a QR code.

Status: the AI Suite was announced on 9 September. AI Forms is still being built, and Nova has no public product page. CCAvenue also announced it has integrated Visa Payment Passkey, so shoppers confirm with a fingerprint or Face ID instead of an OTP.

Why it matters for us: MCP is how gateway features become tools an AI agent can call. Network services that are not exposed this way simply will not appear in those flows.

NPCI · AtOM

Onboarding and certification, as a queue

Today a change to a UPI product — or a new partner joining — travels as specification documents, email threads and spreadsheets between NPCI, each bank and each payment service provider. One change request on the stand's own screen carried 16 documents and 2 open answers.

AtOM runs that exchange as a conversation between agents on each side: four milestones, five decisions that stay with a human, and every message signed and machine-readable so it can be audited later.

Why it matters for us: NPCI describes AtOM for banks and payment service providers, not for merchants — but partner onboarding and certification is exactly where a network either feels easy to work with or does not.
